Welcome to Module 2! Over the next several lessons, we are going to explore how neural networks learn to "see" the world. This field is called **Computer Vision**.

To a computer, an image is not a picture of a cat or a dog. It is a massive grid of numbers.

## The RGB Color Model

Most digital screens emit three colors of light: **R**ed, **G**reen, and **B**lue. By mixing these three lights at different intensities (from 0 to 255), a pixel can display over 16 million different colors.
- White is `[255, 255, 255]`
- Black is `[0, 0, 0]`
- Bright purple is `[255, 0, 255]`

## The 3D Tensor

Because every single pixel requires 3 numbers to describe its color, a standard image cannot be represented as a flat 2D matrix. It must be represented as a **3D Tensor**.

If you have a 1920x1080 high-definition image, the shape of the tensor inside your computer memory is:
`[1080, 1920, 3]`

This means there is a matrix of 1080 rows and 1920 columns, and at each cell, there is an array of 3 numbers.
