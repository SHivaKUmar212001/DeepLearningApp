In 1957, Frank Rosenblatt created the **Perceptron**—the very first artificial neuron. It was originally a physical machine built with wires and motors, designed to recognize simple images. 

Today, it is the fundamental building block of all deep learning.

## How a Neuron Works

A single neuron takes multiple inputs, multiplies each by a **weight**, adds a **bias**, and then decides whether to "fire" or stay silent. 

Mathematically, it computes:

$$ z = w_1 x_1 + w_2 x_2 + b $$

Where:
- $x_1, x_2$ are the **inputs** (what the network sees).
- $w_1, w_2$ are the **weights** (how important each input is).
- $b$ is the **bias** (how easy it is for the neuron to fire).

If $z > 0$, the neuron outputs $1$. If $z \le 0$, the neuron outputs $0$.

## The Decision Boundary

Geometrically, a single neuron with two inputs draws a straight line across 2D space. Everything on one side of the line is classified as `Class 1`, and everything on the other side is `Class 0`.

This line is called the **Decision Boundary**.

### The Limitation of the Perceptron

If the data can be separated by a straight line, it is called **linearly separable**, and a single perceptron can solve it perfectly.

But what if the data forms a circle? Or a spiral? A straight line cannot cut a circle in half perfectly. This is the exact problem that killed neural network research in the 1970s (the "AI Winter"). 

To solve this, we need to bend the straight line. We need **Activation Functions**.
