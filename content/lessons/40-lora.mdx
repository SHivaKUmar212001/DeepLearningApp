We've learned how GPT and BERT scale up to billions of parameters to become extremely intelligent. But how do you train them? 

Training a modern LLM (like Llama 3 with 70 Billion parameters) from scratch costs millions of dollars and requires clusters of thousands of GPUs. 

But what if you just want to *fine-tune* an existing open-source model to learn how to write Python code, or to act like a specific character? You can't afford to update 70 billion parameters on your laptop.

## The Parameter Bottleneck

During backpropagation, we calculate gradients and update the weight matrices. 
A typical weight matrix ($W$) in a Transformer might be $10,000 \times 10,000$. That's **100 million parameters** for a *single* layer!

If you want to fine-tune the model, you have to compute gradients for all 100 million parameters and update them. This requires massive amounts of VRAM that consumer GPUs simply don't have.

## Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)

In 2021, researchers from Microsoft discovered an incredibly elegant mathematical trick called **LoRA**.

Instead of updating the massive frozen matrix $W_0$, what if we just add a small "update matrix" $\Delta W$ to it?
$$ W_{final} = W_0 + \Delta W $$

But $\Delta W$ is still $10,000 \times 10,000$. That doesn't solve the problem!

Here is the trick: Using linear algebra, we can decompose $\Delta W$ into the multiplication of two much smaller matrices, $A$ and $B$.
- Matrix $A$ is $10,000 \times 4$
- Matrix $B$ is $4 \times 10,000$

If we multiply $A$ and $B$, the result is a $10,000 \times 10,000$ matrix! But we only need to store and train the parameters inside $A$ and $B$. 

## The Mathematical Savings

Let's do the math:
- Parameters in $\Delta W$: $100,000,000$
- Parameters in $A$ and $B$: $(10,000 \times 4) + (4 \times 10,000) = 80,000$

We just reduced the number of trainable parameters by **99.92%**!
