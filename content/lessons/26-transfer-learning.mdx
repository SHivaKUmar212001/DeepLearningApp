Training a massive Convolutional Neural Network like ResNet-50 from scratch requires millions of images (like the ImageNet dataset) and weeks of computing power on expensive GPUs. 

What if you want to build an AI to detect a rare disease in medical X-Rays, but you only have 500 images of the disease? If you try to train a CNN from scratch with only 500 images, it will overfit immediately and fail.

The solution is **Transfer Learning**.

## The Universal Feature Extractor

We know that CNNs learn in a hierarchy:
- Early layers learn basic shapes (lines, edges, colors).
- Middle layers learn textures and patterns.
- Late layers learn complex concepts (eyes, wheels).

The magic of Transfer Learning is realizing that **lines, edges, and textures are universal**. The convolutional layers that learned to detect edges of a dog in ImageNet are the exact same mathematical filters needed to detect the edges of a lung in an X-Ray!

## Freezing the Base

To perform Transfer Learning, we do three things:
1. **Download** a massive pre-trained model (like ResNet-50 trained on ImageNet).
2. **Chop off** the final classification head (the Dense layers that output 1000 classes).
3. **Freeze** the convolutional base. We lock all the weights so they cannot change during training.
4. **Attach** a brand new, randomly initialized classification head (e.g., just 2 outputs for "Healthy" vs "Disease").

When we train this new model on our tiny dataset of 500 X-Rays, the gradients *only* update the weights of our new classification head. The massive convolutional base acts purely as a fixed feature extractor!
