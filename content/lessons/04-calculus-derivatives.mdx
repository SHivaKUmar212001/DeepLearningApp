Deep learning is essentially a giant optimization problem: we want to find the exact combination of millions of weights that results in the lowest possible error. But how do we know which way to adjust the weights? We use **calculus**.

## The Derivative
A derivative is the instantaneous **rate of change** of a function. 
If you are driving a car, your position is the function $f(x)$, and your speedometer is the derivative $f'(x)$. 

In deep learning, we don't care about speed; we care about **slope**. The derivative tells us the slope of the loss landscape at any given point.
If the slope is positive, we are walking uphill. If the slope is negative, we are walking downhill. 
By calculating the derivative of the loss with respect to our weights, we know exactly which direction to step to reduce the error.
