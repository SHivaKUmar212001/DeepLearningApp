If an image is just a massive grid of numbers, how do we find a "face" or a "stop sign" inside it? We can't just look at single pixels. A single pixel is just a color. It has no context.

To find patterns, we need to look at **local neighborhoods** of pixels. We do this using a tiny matrix called a **Kernel** (or Filter).

## The 3x3 Kernel

A kernel is simply a small matrix of weights. The most common size is $3 \times 3$.
Instead of connecting every pixel in an image to our neural network (which would take billions of connections), we slide this tiny $3 \times 3$ kernel over the image. 

Depending on the numbers inside the kernel, it will react to different patterns.
- If the kernel has positive numbers on the left and negative numbers on the right, it acts as a **Vertical Edge Detector**.
- If all the numbers are $1$, and we divide by $9$, it acts as a **Blur**.

In traditional computer vision (like Photoshop), engineers had to manually invent these $3 \times 3$ kernels to create effects like "Sharpen" or "Emboss". 

In deep learning, we don't invent the kernels. **The neural network learns the numbers inside the kernels from scratch!** During training, the network might learn that the first kernel should detect horizontal edges, the second should detect vertical edges, and the third should detect diagonal edges.
