We've seen how a CNN can look at an image and output a single probability: "This image contains a dog." But what if there are 3 dogs and 2 cats in the image? How do we find exactly *where* they are?

Welcome to **Object Detection**.

## The Regression Head

To find an object, the network must output the exact coordinates of a **Bounding Box** that surrounds the object. 
Instead of just outputting a probability array at the end of the Dense layers (a Classification Head), we add a second path called a **Regression Head**.

For a single object, the regression head outputs 4 specific numbers:
1. **X Coordinate** (center or top-left)
2. **Y Coordinate** (center or top-left)
3. **Width**
4. **Height**

## Intersection over Union (IoU)

How do we train the network to output the correct coordinates? We use a metric called **Intersection over Union (IoU)**.
During training, we have a "Ground Truth" box drawn by a human. The network guesses a "Prediction" box. 

IoU calculates the area where the two boxes overlap (Intersection) divided by the total area they both cover (Union). 
- An IoU of `0.0` means they don't touch at all.
- An IoU of `1.0` means the prediction perfectly matches the ground truth.

The Loss Function is designed to push the IoU as close to 1.0 as possible!
