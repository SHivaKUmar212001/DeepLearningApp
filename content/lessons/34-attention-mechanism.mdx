In the last lesson, we saw that standard Encoder-Decoder models suffer from a massive bottleneck. The Encoder has to compress a potentially huge sentence into a single, fixed-size Context Vector before handing it to the Decoder. 

If humans were to translate a book, we wouldn't memorize the entire book, close it, and then write the translation from memory. We would translate it sentence by sentence, constantly looking back at the source text for reference.

In 2014, researchers Dzmitry Bahdanau and Thang Luong gave neural networks the ability to "look back" at the source text. This is called **The Attention Mechanism**.

## Dynamic Context Routing

Instead of throwing away all the Encoder's hidden states and only keeping the final one, Attention keeps **all** of them!

When the Decoder is generating the French translation, it doesn't just look at a single Context Vector. It pauses, looks at *every single word* in the English sentence, and calculates an **Attention Score** (between 0% and 100%) for how "relevant" that English word is to the French word it's currently trying to predict.

It then multiplies those scores by the Encoder's hidden states to create a custom, dynamically weighted context vector specifically for that exact moment in time!

## Translating Adjectives

This is incredibly powerful for translating languages with different grammar rules. 

In English, the adjective comes first ("The green apple"). In French, the adjective comes after the noun ("La pomme verte").
