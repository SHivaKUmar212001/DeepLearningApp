Up until now, our neural networks have been entirely **Discriminative**. We feed them an image, and they squeeze it down to a single prediction (e.g., "This is a cat"). 

But what if we want to build a network that creates brand new images? We need to transition from Discriminative AI to **Generative AI**.

The first step on this journey is the **Autoencoder**.

## Learning to Compress

An Autoencoder has two halves:
1. **The Encoder**: Takes a high-dimensional image (e.g., $28 \times 28 = 784$ pixels) and compresses it down into a tiny vector called the **Latent Space**.
2. **The Decoder**: Takes that tiny Latent Space vector and tries to expand it back into the original $784$-pixel image!

Why would we do this? Because forcing the network to squeeze data through a tiny bottleneck forces it to learn the *fundamental underlying concepts* of the data. 

For example, to compress a picture of a handwritten "8", the Encoder might learn to just output `[2 loops, tilted left]`. The Decoder then learns how to draw an "8" from those two abstract concepts.

If the Latent Space is too large (e.g., 128 neurons), the network doesn't actually learn anything useful. It just memorizes the exact pixel values. This is called **Overfitting**.

If the Latent Space is too small (e.g., 2 neurons), the network is forced to learn highly abstract concepts, but it loses too much detail, resulting in a blurry, lossy reconstruction.

## Can We Generate New Images?

You might think: "If the Decoder knows how to turn latent concepts into images, what if I just bypass the Encoder, throw some random numbers into the Latent Space, and see what the Decoder draws?"

Unfortunately, with a standard Autoencoder, this produces absolute garbage. The Latent Space is extremely jagged and full of empty "holes" where the Decoder has no idea what to draw.
