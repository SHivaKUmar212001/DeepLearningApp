Neural networks are mathematical functions. They take numbers as input, multiply them by weights, and output numbers. They have absolutely no concept of English, Spanish, or what a "word" is.

So, how do we feed a book into a neural network? We must translate language into numbers. This process is called **Tokenization**.

## The Vocabulary

To tokenize text, we first need a dictionary of all known words, called a **Vocabulary**. 
Every word in the vocabulary is assigned a unique integer ID.

- `the` $\rightarrow 101$
- `cat` $\rightarrow 842$
- `sat` $\rightarrow 305$

The sentence "the cat sat" becomes the 1D tensor: `[101, 842, 305]`.

## The Problem with Words

If our vocabulary contains every word in the English language, it will be massive (hundreds of thousands of words). But what happens if the user types a word the network has never seen before, like `DeepDiveApp`?

If a word isn't in the vocabulary, it's considered **Out Of Vocabulary (OOV)**, and is usually mapped to an "Unknown" token ID like `[UNK]`. This means the network loses all information about that word.

## Subword Tokenization (BPE)

To solve the OOV problem, modern models like GPT don't use whole words. They use **Subword Tokenization** (like Byte-Pair Encoding or BPE).

Instead of mapping `jumped` to a single ID, they split it into its root and suffix: `jump` and `##ed`. 
This is brilliant because:
1. The vocabulary stays small (usually around ~30,000 to ~50,000 tokens).
2. The network can understand brand new words! If you type `Bitcoinizing`, it might split it into `Bit` + `coin` + `##ize` + `##ing`, allowing the network to mathematically guess its meaning from the roots.
