In the Reverse Process, we need a neural network to look at a noisy $512 \times 512$ image and predict the noise at every single pixel. 

Because we need an output that is the exact same size as the input, we could try using a massive grid of standard Convolutional layers. However, that is far too slow and takes too much memory. 

Instead, Diffusion Models use a famous architecture borrowed from medical imaging: the **U-Net**.

## The Autoencoder Structure

A U-Net is structured very much like an Autoencoder, shaped like a "U".

1. **Downsampling (Encoder)**: The left side of the U compresses the image. It halves the spatial resolution while doubling the number of feature channels. This allows the network to "zoom out" and understand the **global context** (e.g., "The noise roughly forms the shape of a dog").
2. **The Bottleneck**: At the bottom of the U, the image is highly compressed. The network has maximum global context but has lost all fine pixel details.
3. **Upsampling (Decoder)**: The right side of the U expands the abstract bottleneck back up to a $512 \times 512$ image.

## The Skip Connections

If the U-Net was just an Autoencoder, it would output a blurry, low-resolution noise prediction because the Bottleneck lost all the sharp edges. 

The genius of the U-Net is the **Skip Connections**.

At every level of the U, a Skip Connection copies the high-resolution feature maps from the Downsampling path and directly attaches them to the Upsampling path!

This provides the Decoder with the exact, sharp local details it needs, while still benefiting from the global context learned in the Bottleneck. 

The U-Net is the perfect engine for Diffusion. But currently, if we give it pure noise, it will just generate a random image. How do we force it to draw a *specific* image, like "a cute cat"?
