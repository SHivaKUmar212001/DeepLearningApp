We've spent the entire module seeing how powerful Neural Networks are at finding patterns in images. However, this power comes with a terrifying vulnerability. 

Because neural networks look at images as massive mathematical tensors (not as visual concepts like humans do), they can be tricked by math.

## The FGSM Attack

The **Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM)** is a classic adversarial attack. 
Normally, during training, we use Backpropagation to find the gradients of the *Weights* with respect to the Loss, so we can update the weights to make the model smarter.

In an FGSM attack, the attacker does the exact opposite. They freeze the model's weights, and instead calculate the gradient of the *Image* with respect to the Loss. 
They ask the math: *"How do I change the pixels in this image just slightly to absolutely maximize the chance the network predicts 'Gibbon' instead of 'Panda'?"*

## Invisible Noise

The attacker calculates a specific noise pattern tensor. They multiply it by a tiny number ($\epsilon$) so it becomes almost invisible to the human eye, and add it to the original image tensor.

To you and me, the image still looks perfectly like a Panda. 
But to the neural network, those tiny pixel changes align perfectly with the high-dimensional mathematical decision boundary of a "Gibbon". The model's confidence completely flips!
