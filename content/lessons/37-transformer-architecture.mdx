In 2017, researchers from Google Brain published a paper titled **"Attention Is All You Need"**. 

They proposed completely throwing away all Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for language modeling. Instead, they built an architecture entirely out of Self-Attention and standard Feed Forward networks.

They called it **The Transformer**.

## Bringing It All Together

The Transformer is an Encoder-Decoder model, just like we saw in Lesson 33. But because it doesn't use RNNs, it has no Sequence Bottleneck. The Encoder can look at the entire 500-word source essay simultaneously, and the Decoder can cross-attend to all 500 words at once while translating!

This architecture powers almost every major AI breakthrough today. Let's look at the complete block diagram of the original Transformer.

## The Legacy of the Transformer

The original Transformer was built for translation (English to French). It needed both an Encoder (to read English) and a Decoder (to generate French).

But soon after, researchers started asking:
- What if we just rip the **Encoder** out and use it by itself to understand text? (This became Google's **BERT**).
- What if we just rip the **Decoder** out and use it by itself to generate text? (This became OpenAI's **GPT**).

In the final lessons of this module, we will explore these two massive branching paths!
