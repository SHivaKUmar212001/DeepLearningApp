Q-Learning and DQNs are **Value-Based** methods. They train a neural network to look at a State and predict exactly how much Reward it will get for taking a specific Action.

But what if the exact value of an action is impossible to calculate? For example, in a game of Mario, what is the exact numerical value of jumping over a Goomba? It's extremely hard to quantify.

Instead of guessing the *value* of an action, what if we just trained the neural network to output the *probability* of taking that action? This is called a **Policy-Based** method, and the algorithm is called **Policy Gradients**.

## The Policy Network $\pi_\theta$

A Policy Network takes in a State (like the pixels of a video game) and outputs a probability distribution over all possible Actions. 

For example, if Mario is running towards a Goomba, the network might output:
- Jump: 33%
- Duck: 33%
- Run: 33%

The Agent then rolls a 3-sided die (weighted by those probabilities) and takes an action.

## The Gradient Update

Let's say the Agent rolls the die and randomly decides to "Jump". It jumps over the Goomba and the Environment gives it a Reward of +10.

Because we are doing Deep Learning, we can calculate the **Gradients**! We use backpropagation to slightly tweak the weights of the neural network so that the probability of "Jump" increases, and the probability of "Duck" and "Run" decreases.

## Why Policy Gradients?

Policy Gradients are incredibly stable because they smoothly adjust probabilities rather than drastically changing absolute value numbers. 

They are the foundation for the most advanced Reinforcement Learning algorithms in the world today, such as **PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization)**, which is exactly how OpenAI trained ChatGPT to be polite and helpful using human feedback (RLHF)!
