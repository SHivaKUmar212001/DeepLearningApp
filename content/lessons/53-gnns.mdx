Throughout this course, we've looked at two main types of data:
1. **Grids**: Images are perfect 2D grids of pixels. We use Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for these.
2. **Sequences**: Text is a 1D sequence of words. We use Transformers or RNNs for these.

But what if the data doesn't fit into a perfect grid or a neat sequence? What if the data is a complex web of connections? 

Think about a social network, a molecule, or a traffic system. These are called **Graphs**. To process them, we need **Graph Neural Networks (GNNs)**.

## Nodes and Edges

A Graph consists of two things:
- **Nodes**: The entities (e.g., people in a social network, atoms in a molecule).
- **Edges**: The connections between them (e.g., friendships, chemical bonds).

Each node has its own "Feature Vector" (a list of numbers describing it). But simply looking at a single node's features isn't enough. To understand a node, you have to understand who its friends are!

## Message Passing

The core algorithm of a GNN is called **Message Passing**.

In every layer of a GNN, each node takes its Feature Vector and sends it along its edges to all of its direct neighbors. It also receives the vectors from all of its neighbors.

## Aggregation

Once a node receives all the incoming "messages" from its neighbors, it has to combine them. This is called **Aggregation**. The simplest way to aggregate is to just sum them all together, but modern GNNs use complex neural networks to intelligently combine the messages.

After one round of Message Passing, Node A knows about Node B. 
But after *two* rounds of Message Passing, Node B sends its newly updated information back to Node A. This means Node A now knows about Node B's friends (Nodes C and D)!

With every layer of the GNN, the nodes learn about an increasingly wider neighborhood of the graph.

## Applications

GNNs are rapidly becoming one of the most important fields in Deep Learning:
- **Drug Discovery**: GNNs can look at a graph of a new molecule and predict if it will cure a disease or be toxic.
- **Google Maps**: GNNs predict estimated arrival times by running message passing over the global road network.
- **Recommendation Systems**: Pinterest uses a massive GNN with billions of nodes to recommend pins by finding paths between users and images.
