By switching from RNNs to Self-Attention, we gained the ability to process an entire sentence simultaneously in parallel. This made training massive models incredibly fast on GPUs.

But we lost something critical: **Time**.

## The Bag of Words Problem

A Self-Attention mechanism looks at every word at the exact same time. It has absolutely no concept of order. 
To a raw Self-Attention layer, "The dog chased the cat" and "The cat chased the dog" look mathematically identical. It's just a bag of words!

To fix this, we have to manually inject a sense of "position" or "time" into the words *before* we feed them into the network.

## The Positional Encoding Matrix

In the original Transformer paper, the researchers decided to add a unique mathematical signature to each word based on its position in the sentence.

They didn't just use integers like `1, 2, 3`. They used a brilliant combination of **Sine** and **Cosine** waves of varying frequencies.

Why waves? 
Because waves are continuous and periodic. This allows the network to easily learn *relative* positions. For example, the network can mathematically figure out that word 5 and word 7 are exactly 2 steps apart, regardless of how long the sentence is.

This matrix is literally **added** to the Word Embeddings. 

$$ FinalEmbedding = WordEmbedding + PositionalEncoding $$

Now, every word in the sentence contains a unique, high-dimensional timestamp. The Transformer can finally understand order!
