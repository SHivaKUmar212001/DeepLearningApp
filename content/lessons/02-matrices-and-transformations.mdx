If a vector is a point or an arrow in space, a **matrix** is an action that transforms that space. 

In deep learning, every Linear layer (or Dense layer) is mathematically just a matrix multiplication. When we pass an input vector $x$ into a layer with weights $W$, we compute:

$$ y = Wx $$

What does this actually mean geometrically?

## Matrices as Linear Transformations

A $2 \times 2$ matrix tells us exactly where the fundamental grid lines of space (the basis vectors) will land.
Let's call the unit vector pointing right $\hat{i} = [1, 0]$ and the unit vector pointing up $\hat{j} = [0, 1]$.

A matrix $W = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}$ dictates that:
- The new $\hat{i}$ lands at $[a, c]$
- The new $\hat{j}$ lands at $[b, d]$

Every other point in space follows them, stretching and rotating the grid.

### The Determinant

Notice the **Determinant** readout. The determinant measures the factor by which the *area* of the grid squares scales.
- **Determinant = 1**: The area is unchanged (e.g., Rotation).
- **Determinant > 1**: Space is stretched outward.
- **Determinant < 0**: Space is flipped inside out (like a mirror reflection).
- **Determinant = 0**: Space collapses into a single line (1D) or a point (0D). This means the matrix destroys information—you cannot reverse it!

In neural networks, weight matrices constantly stretch, rotate, and squash the input data into new representations until the classes become linearly separable.
