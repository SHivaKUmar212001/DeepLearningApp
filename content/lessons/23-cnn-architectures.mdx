We now have all the individual building blocks of a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN):
1. **Convolutions** to extract features and build depth.
2. **ReLU** activations to introduce non-linearity.
3. **Pooling Layers** to downsample space and build translational invariance.

How do we actually stack these blocks together to build a model that can recognize handwritten digits (LeNet), classify 1000 different objects (AlexNet, VGG), or even drive a car?

## The CNN Funnel

Classic CNN architectures look like a massive mathematical funnel. As the data flows deeper into the network, two things happen simultaneously:

1. **Spatial Dimensions Shrink**: Thanks to Pooling layers and Strided convolutions, the physical width and height of the feature maps shrink from something like $224 \times 224$ down to $7 \times 7$.
2. **Channel Depth Grows**: Thanks to having dozens or hundreds of different convolutional kernels per layer, the number of channels (feature maps) grows from $3$ (RGB) to hundreds or thousands (e.g., $512$).

The network transforms a wide, thin image into a narrow, incredibly deep block of highly abstract features.

## The fully connected tail

Once the data has been funneled into a deep block of abstract features, we **Flatten** it. We take the 3D tensor and unroll it into a single 1D vector. 

This vector is then fed into standard **Dense (Fully Connected)** layers—exactly like the MLP we built in Module 1. The convolutions do the hard work of feature extraction, and the dense layers do the final classification!
