The **Learning Rate** (often denoted by $\eta$ or $\alpha$) is arguably the most important hyperparameter in deep learning. It determines the size of the step the optimizer takes during gradient descent.

If the learning rate is too small, your model will take an eternity to train, or it might get stuck in a shallow local minimum. If the learning rate is too high, the optimizer will wildly bounce across the loss surface and might even diverge (loss goes to infinity).

### The Plateau Problem
As a model trains, it often hits **plateaus**—regions where the loss stops decreasing because the gradient is very small, but it hasn't reached the true minimum yet. A constant learning rate that was perfect at the beginning of training is often too large to settle into the fine details of the minimum at the end of training.

### Learning Rate Schedules
Instead of keeping the learning rate constant, we **schedule** it to decrease over time:
1. **Step Decay**: Drop the learning rate by a factor (e.g., divide by 2) every $N$ epochs.
2. **Exponential Decay**: Continuously multiply the learning rate by a decay factor less than 1.
3. **Cosine Annealing**: Start high and slowly drop to near-zero following the shape of a cosine curve. This is currently the most popular method for training large language models.
